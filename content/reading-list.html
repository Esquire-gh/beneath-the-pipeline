Notes on the list

Cite Designing Data-Intensive Applications by edition. The second edition (Kleppmann & Riccomini, 2026) renumbered the chapters from the first, so this list references them by title rather than by number.

Two stages have no book. Parsing has a specification and a research literature, and module 13 says so on the page rather than pretending otherwise. That absence is itself informative: the pipeline line that decides the most has the least settled theory behind it.

What is deliberately not here. Growth beyond one machine, meaning partitioning, replication, and distributed query fanout, is out of scope for this site. Everything here runs on one computer, and that constraint is what keeps the mechanisms visible. Designing Data-Intensive Applications's replication and sharding chapters are the named next step for anyone who finishes.

If you only read three

In this order, and each one after the module that earns it:

  1. Introduction to Information Retrieval, chapters 1–2 and 6–8. Free online. It is the spine of Part II, and chapter 8 will make you more careful about every quality claim you ever read.
  2. Computer Systems: A Programmer's Perspective, chapter 2. One chapter that permanently fixes what a number is. Everything in Part I stands on it.
  3. Designing Data-Intensive Applications, 2nd edition, opening chapters. The formal statement of module 14's argument: the shape of your data decides which questions you can ask.
