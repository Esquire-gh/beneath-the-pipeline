Beneath the Pipeline

A retrieval pipeline is {{ 00-pipeline.pipeline_lines.code_lines }} lines of Python sitting on decades of storage engineering, byte-level convention, and information-retrieval research. You are about to build every one of those floors yourself, at small scale, and then push each one until it breaks.

0a · The pipeline, working

Here is the pipeline the internet teaches. Open PDFs, pull out their text, cut the text into chunks, turn each chunk into a list of numbers, put those numbers in a database, and ask the database for the four chunks nearest your question.

0b · The inventory of assumptions

Walk the pipeline line by line. Each line does something you could not explain to someone who asked. Here is the literal list.

This list is the table of contents. Every question below names the module that answers it, and the modules are in pipeline order.

  • load(pdf_dir)

    What is a file? Your program asked for one and got bytes back. Where were those bytes, what told the machine where to find them, and what did the asking cost?

    And why do the bytes on disk become text at all? Who decided that this byte means that letter?

    01 · what is a file 02 · how text becomes bytes 04 · load
  • parse(docs)

    Why is extracting text from a PDF a step? The text is right there on the screen. Why does a program have to work for it?

    And why do two libraries reading the same file disagree about what it says?

    05 · parse 13 · why parsing is hard
  • split(parsed, size=1000)

    Why cut the text up at all? Who chose 1000, and what happens to a table that the cut lands in the middle of?

    06 · chunk & embed
  • embed(chunks, model)

    How did text become {{ 00-pipeline.dims }} numbers? What is a number here, how is it stored, and how exact is it?

    And what does distance between two of these lists of numbers actually promise you?

    03 · how numbers become bytes 06 · chunk & embed 12 · approximation
  • db.add(...)

    What did the database build? Something was constructed, it took time, and it takes up room. What is it, and why does having it make asking for neighbours fast?

    What happens when it no longer fits in memory, or when new documents arrive after it was built?

    07 · index 09 · it doesn't fit 10 · ingestion vs query
  • retrieve(db, model, q, k=4)

    Top four, ranked by what? Say a query matches four thousand passages. Four come back. Which four, and who decided?

    How would you know if the ranking is bad? What does it cost to rank a million passages instead of a hundred thousand?

    And the last assumption, the one nobody states: is "the nearest chunk" even the right kind of answer to every question?

    08 · retrieve 11 · at scale 14 · what vectors can't answer

The contract

By the end of this site you will have rebuilt these {{ 00-pipeline.pipeline_lines.code_lines }} lines yourself, from parts you wrote. You will know what every one of them costs. And you will know the questions they cannot answer.

The order never varies. Part I lays the floor: what a disk holds, what a file is, and how numbers and text become bytes by agreement. Part II walks the pipeline one stage at a time and has you build an honest small version of each. Part III turns the assumptions off, first scale and then the two hard truths the demo hides, and the last module puts your own parts back together and measures them against documents that fight back.

meaning is a convention; arrangement is a cost.

That sentence is the whole site. Every module is one more place where it is true. Start at the floor.
